# Accounts and Parental Controls

One household, one owner, and as many sub-accounts as you want. Every account has its own
PIN, its own permissions, and its own view of the library.

Accounts live under **Users**.

## Roles

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | Everything. Bypasses every permission check. Exactly one per install, enforced by a database constraint |
| **Admin** | Edit, reset the PIN of, unlock, and force-logout an existing sub-account, plus admin-only settings, emulator, and BIOS endpoints. **Cannot create or delete accounts** |
| **Sub-account** | Whatever its permission flags allow |

`is_admin` grants nothing else implicitly. Every other flag is checked independently.

## Permission flags

Only the owner can grant these.

| Flag | Controls | Default for a new sub-account |
|---|---|---|
| `can_launch_media` | Launching anything | On |
| `can_manage_game` | Adding, editing, deleting, scanning, and importing games, and managing launch profiles | Off |
| `can_manage_media` | The archival Media domain | Off |
| `can_manage_app` | The Apps domain, including its uploads | Off |
| `can_manage_environment` | Registering and modifying Environments | Off |
| `can_manage_controllerMapping` | Controller mappings | Off |
| `can_manage_settings` | Reading and changing application settings | Off |
| `can_manage_users` | Editing **its own** name and resetting **its own** PIN. Nothing else | Off |

`can_manage_users` is deliberately narrow. It grants no capability over any other account,
no self-delete, and none of the owner-only operations.

## PINs

- 4 to 6 digits, hashed with Argon2id and a per-user random salt. Plaintext PINs are never
  stored, logged, or returned by any endpoint.
- **Four consecutive wrong attempts locks the account.** The count-and-lock is one atomic
  database update, so two simultaneous attempts cannot both slip past the threshold.
- The switch endpoint is additionally rate limited to 30 attempts per minute per source IP.
  That budget is deliberately loose, because several accounts on a household network share
  one IP. The per-account four-attempt lockout is the real brake on guessing a single PIN;
  the IP cap exists to stop high-volume sweeps across many accounts.
- The **owner always needs its PIN**, even to switch back into an already-active session.

### Optional pepper

You can set an app-level `PIN_PEPPER` in Settings. It is stored in `.env`, outside the
database, and mixed directly into the hash.

:::warning Setting or changing the pepper is disruptive by design
Every previously-issued hash becomes unverifiable. All non-owner accounts are forced back
into "PIN required" with their hash cleared, and the owner is re-hashed in the same request
using the PIN you supply. There is no migration path and no way to rotate it without this.
Treat it as a communicated event, not a casual toggle.
:::

## Resetting a PIN

| Caller | Can reset |
|---|---|
| Owner | Anyone's, regardless of lock state or role |
| Admin | Regular sub-accounts only. Not the owner, not another admin, not itself |
| `can_manage_users` holder | Its own only, and **not** while its account is locked |
| Anyone | **Never the owner's** |

A PIN reset does not end existing sessions. Use **Force logout** to revoke one.

### Owner lockout recovery

The only recovery path is local:

```bash
python scripts/setup_admin_user.py
```

It prompts for a name and new PIN, overwrites the owner record, re-grants every permission,
clears the lock, and revokes any session issued before the reset. There is no remote reset,
no email recovery, and no bypass flag. Packaged builds ship `reset_owner.bat` as a wrapper.

## Content ratings

Each sub-account has a rating ceiling and a switch for unrated content.

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| `max_content_rating` | Items rated above this are **hidden entirely**, not shown and then refused at launch |
| `block_unrated_media` | Whether items with no rating are visible |

Ratings are read at scan time from NFO sidecars, disc metadata, or the filename, and the
owner can override any of them. The scale is free text; ESRB (E, E10+, T, M, AO) and PEGI
(3, 7, 12, 16, 18) are the recommended vocabularies.

**The filter fails closed.** An item whose rating cannot be recognised is denied to a capped
account rather than passed through. If an account's own ceiling stops resolving to a known
value, no rated content passes at all rather than the cap being silently dropped.
Enforcement is server side, deny wins over every permission flag, and there is no override
path.

### Per-item restrictions

Separately from ratings, an admin can block specific games, media, or apps for specific
accounts. An admin who is themselves blocked from an item cannot read or edit its
restriction list either.

## Sessions

- One active session per account, by design. Signing in on a second device ends the first.
- Sessions do not expire server-side by default. You can set a per-account time-to-live.
- The session cookie is `HttpOnly` and `SameSite=Lax`, and the browser-side lifetime is
  capped at 30 days even when the server-side session never expires.
- Every state-changing request carries a CSRF token, so another browser tab cannot act on
  your behalf.

Deleting a sub-account removes its per-item restrictions and reassigns its launch profiles
to the owner. The profiles are not deleted.
